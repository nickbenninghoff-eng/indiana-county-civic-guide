# Neutrality review 2: St. Joseph County Edition (v1.0-draft, build of 2026-11-08)

Reviewer: neutrality QA agent, 2026-11-08. Read-only review; the only file written is this one.

**Why a second pass.** The About page now says the author works as an organizer for the St. Joseph County Democratic Party. That raises the bar: a Republican reader will start out assuming slant, and a Democratic reader will watch for over-correction. This pass covers what was added or changed today: the "How areas voted" map layer, party tags and body breakdowns, the "At a glance" boxes, the disclosure and footer, the county litigation text, the landing page, picker and search, and the three looks.

**Method.** I pulled the text out of all 60 built pages in `site/st-joseph/` with the tags stripped. Then I read the code and data behind the new features: `tools/site/civic/map.py`, `answers.py`, `search.py`, `components.py` (party helpers), `static/js/map_votes.js`, `search.js`, `static/css/map_votes.css`, `map.css`, `party.css`, `looks.css`, `tools/build_map.py`, `data/editions/st-joseph/derived/map_votes.json`, `precinct_results.csv`, `edition.json` and `sources_gov.csv`. I recomputed the county-wide margins for every race in `precinct_results.csv`. I counted current holders by party and confidence through `civic.data.Edition` and `term_party()`, and I computed OKLab distances for every map and look color against the party colors and the vote-band tints. I used 2 of the 10 allowed web searches, both on the litigation.

**Severity.** BLOCKER = must fix before sharing. FIX = should change. NOTE = defensible but could be tighter.
**Persona.** R = skeptical Republican hunting for pro-Democratic slant. D = skeptical Democrat hunting for pro-Republican slant. Both = both would object.

## Checked and found fine

- **Party tag rules** (`components.py`). Codes are in A–Z order, never by count. "not recorded", "nonpartisan" and "holder not listed" come after the party codes in a fixed order. Every dot has a text label, and the abbr title spells out the party.
- **Unknown and low-confidence handling doesn't lean either way.** Current holders: 68 R, 50 D, 38 NP, 1 O, 6 blank. All 6 blanks are town officials (the New Carlisle council ×3, the New Carlisle, North Liberty and Roseland clerk-treasurers), each with "Party not found/stated" in the source notes. No current holder shows "(R?)" or "(D?)". The six low-confidence current rows are all NP judges and school board members, and they correctly show "(nonpartisan ballot)".
- **Breakdowns match across pages.** County (1 D, 2 R / 4 D, 5 R), Mishawaka (7 D, 2 R), South Bend (9 D), townships and towns are the same on the landing, map, place pages and county page.
- **Vote layer mechanics.** Bands are symmetric (5/15/30 for both parties). The "Under 5 points either way" band is gray. "D +x" and "R +x" avoid negative numbers. Libertarian and other votes are in the totals and shares and explained. The caveat ("They show how votes were cast here, not how any person or future election will go") appears in the panel, the idle state and the tables. The layer is off by default and absent from the landing map. Unplaced post-2020 precincts are listed openly and their votes are small and mixed (for example Gov 2024, 090508: 298 D / 224 R; 090308: 133 D / 199 R). The panel lists parties in a fixed order, not by who won.
- **Loaded-term lint** on landing, map, county, about and every place page: clean.
- **Place pages:** review 1's F6 is fixed. Budget now sits between Recent results and Context on every template.
- **Party-control charts:** review 1's B7 is fixed. Commissioners 2013-2020 show 1 D · 2 R.
- **Search** (`search.py`, `search.js`): party tags are stored as display text only and aren't searchable. Ranking is by match score, then type, then name. Party plays no part.
- **Picker and places index:** alphabetical within cities, towns and townships, and stated as such. No party data in the picker.
- **Landing copy:** neutral ("One address. At least 5 governments..."). Map caption: "Shading shows the type of government only, never party" (but see F4).
- **Ballot order** is now explained on campaign finance ("Why one party comes first ...") and on schools (party order follows the last Secretary of State race), which settles review 1's N4.
- **Looks:** the edition teal (ΔE 16.7 from D), purples (ΔE 17-19 from D) and browns/olive (ΔE 20-21 from R) clear the stated ΔE ≥ 15 against the full party colors. Party tokens aren't redefined per look. The About text on looks is accurate. The exception is the map tints in F4.
- **Footer disclosure** appears on all 60 pages with a link to the full statement.
- **County litigation:** the news-sourced claims are labeled with outlet and date ("ABC57 reported on Apr 22, 2026 ...", "95.3 MNC reported on May 14, 2026 ..."), and the page says "The order's text is not posted publicly, so the scope described above comes from the news reports." The court record facts (dates, statutes, appeal number, briefing status) come from MyCase. The opt-in resolution (R-16-C-2023, July 11, 2023, 3-0) is stated without characterization.

## Verdict

**Skeptical Republican: not yet.** The structural rules hold up: tags, ordering, unknown handling and search are clean and symmetric. The new map layer is where this reader will push. The race menu drops the two 2022 races Republicans carried and defaults to the widest 2024 Democratic margin (B1). It ships a precinct-level partisan map the About page says the guide doesn't make (B2). The lawsuit text gives only the plaintiffs' side and doesn't name the county defendants (B3). Each of these can be fixed in an afternoon. Once they are, the town "who's in charge" fix (F3) and the disclosure details (F1, F2) are what remain.

**Skeptical Democrat: mostly yes.** The main objection is that coloring by land area makes a county the Democrat carried in four of the seven mapped races look mostly red, with no county total shown (F5). The color-cue problem (F4) cuts both ways.

Both readers need a working corrections contact (B4) before the link goes out.
