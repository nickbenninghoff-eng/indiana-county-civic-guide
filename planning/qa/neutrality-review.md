# Neutrality review: St. Joseph County Edition (v1.0-draft, data as of 2026-11-07)

Reviewer: neutrality QA agent, 2026-11-08. Read-only review; nothing was edited.

**Method.** I read `planning/ux-review.md` §1.4 and §6, `data/SCHEMA.md` and the selection rules on `history/people/`. Then I pulled the text and chart data out of all 59 built pages in `site/st-joseph/`. The priority pages (landing, county, history, history/people, community/research-on-voting, elections/landscape, money/campaigns, state/your-legislators, about, teach) were read in full. Every other page was linted for loaded terms and party narrative and spot-read. Where a page and the data disagreed, I checked against `data/editions/st-joseph/` (`terms.csv`, `people.csv`, `timeline.csv`, `cf_local.csv`). I used 3 web searches to check factual premises (Beutter's party, Zakas's tenure, the commissioner-district ruling). Sources are at the end.

**Loaded-term lint.** Clean. No banned term appears in the guide's own text. The only hits are quoted source headlines in the source lists ("Democrats sweep major St. Joseph County races", "Indiana Democrats break GOP supermajority in House"), which the About policy allows. See N7.

**Severity.** BLOCKER = a reasonable reader would see bias. FIX = should change. NOTE = defensible but could be tighter.
**Persona.** R = skeptical local Republican. D = skeptical local Democrat. Both = both would object.

## What the guide does well (keep these)

- **Mechanical ordering and labeling.** Officeholders are listed by office and district everywhere, with the party label written out in full ("Democrat (D)"). Incoming 2026 winners are clearly separated and marked UNOFFICIAL.
- **Full-range charts.** Party control, turnout and populations all show the full range of the data, with "no data" and series notes instead of a chosen start year. Turnout always shows its denominator, and odd and even years are visually separated.
- **Elections by the numbers is a model of symmetry.** Margins are defined once, with three bands applied to 331 races sorted closest first. There is no party filter, no safe/target vocabulary and an explicit "What this page does not do" box.
- **Campaign finance.** No ranking by money, ballot order within races, side-by-side figures only when the periods match exactly, a coverage matrix before any figures, FILER-INCONSISTENT stated without guessing at reasons, and both county party committees in the data. The "money is not a measure of support" framing is right.
- **Attribution of contested "firsts" in both directions.** WNDU's "first Republican majority" and WVPE's "first Democratic board majority since 2012" each get the same "not independently verified" treatment, and the guide checks both against the canvass.
- **Published skew.** The people page shows the party/era count and says plainly that Republicans are under-represented and why. That is exactly what §6 asked for. The fixes above make the numbers better, not the disclosure.
- **Ecological-fallacy design.** The callout, no demographic overlays on results, the teach-page prompt and the "places, not people" language are consistent across pages.
- **Gaps on every page.** They are specific about what is missing and how to fill it. This is the guide's strongest trust feature.
- **Clean wording.** The banned-term lint passes across all 59 pages, and the teach page's "How prompts are written" rules are good.
- **Photos.** They follow a license rule with a uniform no-photo panel, and portraits appear only on History.

## Verdict

**Skeptical Republican: not yet.** The rules and the structure are fair, but where they are *applied* to people and party history, the errors all fall the same way. Hiler is excluded against the HIGHER rule as written, Wesco is missing against TENURE, the turning-point rule picks up a Democratic council member but not two Republican commissioners, sitting officials get cards (6 D, 2 R), the Yakym card is lopsided, and the party-control charts hide documented Republican seats on two bodies. Each of these is a one-screenshot argument. All of them can be fixed mechanically, with data the guide already holds or one gap-fill pass (Zakas, Mishawaka parties). With B1-B7 and F12/F13 fixed, this reader has little left to object to beyond the ordinary NOTEs.

**Skeptical Democrat: mostly yes, with two objections.** The elections, money and community layers would satisfy this reader. They will object to the 2021 redistricting account (B8), which leaves out the commissioners' first move and pins the legal costs on the Democrats' lawsuit, and to the city litigation placed on Buttigieg's card (F3). The refusal to analyze place and voting is defensible and explained (N11). Once B8, F3 and F14 are fixed, this reader should accept the guide as fair.

Both readers need the author disclosure (B9) before they would treat any of it as nonpartisan.
