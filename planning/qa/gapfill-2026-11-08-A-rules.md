## 2. Early-voting Saturdays - FILLED

IC 3-11-10-26 (https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-3/article-11/chapter-10/section-3-11-10-26/, 2025 Indiana Code, last amended by P.L.186-2025 and P.L.240-2025; read 2026-11-08):
- (f): "The voter may vote before the board not more than twenty-eight (28) days nor later than noon on the day before election day."
- (h): "The absentee voter board in the office of the circuit court clerk must permit voters to cast absentee ballots under this section for at least seven (7) hours on each of the two (2) Saturdays preceding election day." The county election board may by resolution authorize the clerk (at the clerk's office or a satellite office) to permit voting "for at least four (4) hours on the third Saturday preceding election day."
- (i): "in a county with a population of less than twenty thousand (20,000)" the board, with election-board approval, "may reduce the number of hours ... to a minimum of four (4) hours on each of the two (2) Saturdays preceding election day."
Conclusion: two Saturdays of at least 7 hours each are required statewide, with a 4-hour minimum allowed only in counties under 20,000 people (St. Joseph is far above that). The third Saturday (4 hours) is optional by resolution. This is the clerk's-office and satellite-office absentee voter board rule. Confidence high. The guide's line "every county must offer the two Saturdays" is right but should add the under-20,000 exception.

## 5. Eligibility for local offices (IC 3-8-1) - PARTLY (mostly filled)

Source: sections of https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-3/article-8/chapter-1/ (2025 Indiana Code, read 2026-11-08).
- 3-8-1-1(b): "A person is not qualified to run for: (1) a state office; (2) a legislative office; (3) a local office; or (4) a school board office; unless the person is registered to vote in the election district the person seeks to represent not later than the deadline for filing the declaration or petition of candidacy or certificate of nomination." Exempt: judge of a city court and judge of a town court (3-8-1-1(a)).
- 3-8-1-20 (county auditor, recorder, treasurer, sheriff, coroner, surveyor): resided in the county at least one year before the election (Ind. Const. Art. 6 Sec. 4).
- 3-8-1-21 (county commissioner): one year in the county and six months in the district before the election.
- 3-8-1-22 (county council): one year in the county; "in the district in which seeking election, if applicable, for at least six (6) months."
- 3-8-1-26 (mayor of a second or third class city): resided in the city at least one year before the election.
- 3-8-1-27 (common council, second or third class city): city one year; district (if applicable) six months, before the election.
- 3-8-1-28 (city clerk of a second class city): city one year.
- 3-8-1-29 (town council): "must reside in the district in which seeking election, if applicable." No length stated.
- 3-8-1-34 (school board): school corporation one year before the election; if the seat is for an election district smaller than the corporation, the election district one year.
- 3-8-1-32 (precinct committeeman): "must comply with any candidate requirement set by state party rules" (for parties whose nominee got at least 10% of the Secretary of State vote).
- Age: none of these sections states a minimum age (voter registration implies 18). Not separately sourced.
- Not read this pass: 3-8-1-19.5 (circuit court clerk), 3-8-1-23 (county assessor), 3-8-1-3 to 3-8-1-5 (lucrative offices, collectors of public money, disqualification), township trustee/board residency (IC 36-6-4-2 already in the data), town clerk-treasurer. Chalkbeat (May 11, 2026) says a teacher or other district employee cannot serve on the same board but can run if they leave the job before taking office (secondary; not statute-checked).
Confidence high for the quoted sections. 2026-session amendments not checked.

## 6. Petition signatures (IC 3-8-6-3) - FILLED

(b) ... any fraction in excess of a whole number must be disregarded. Confidence high.

## 7. Local campaign finance reporting (IC 3-9-5) - FILLED

Sources: sections of https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-3/article-9/chapter-5/ (2025 Indiana Code, read 2026-11-08) and the Election Division 2026 Election Calendar, Election Administrators Edition, revised March 2026 (https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/files/2026-Election-Calendar-Election-Administrators-Edition-Revised-March-2026.pdf).
- 3-9-5-4: candidates for local office and their committees, and candidates for school board office and their committees, file with "the county election board of each county comprising part of the affected election district." (amended by P.L.240-2025 SEC.20)
- 3-9-5-6(a) (non-state candidate committees): report current and dated as of "(1) Twenty-five (25) days before the nomination date. (2) Twenty-five (25) days before the general, municipal, or special election. (3) The annual report filed and dated as required by section 10."
- 3-9-5-7(b): reports "must be filed ... not later than noon seven (7) days after the date of the report." So a pre-election report is due at noon 18 days before the election. Example in the calendar: the 2026 pre-primary period ended Friday April 10, 2026 and the report was due "not later than noon, April 17, 2026."
- 3-9-5-10(a): the annual report is "complete as of December 31 of the previous year" and due "by noon the third Wednesday in January" for a candidate's committee (March 1 for regular party committees). Calendar: the 2026 annual report is due noon Wednesday January 20, 2027.
- 3-9-5-9(d): a city or town candidate files on the section 6 schedule "as if the primary were conducted" even when no municipal primary is held. 3-9-5-9(a): in a year the candidate is not on the ballot, only the annual report is required.
- Forms (calendar): CFA-4 = "Report of Receipts and Expenditures of a Political Committee". CFA-11 = Supplemental "Large Contribution" Report (IC 3-9-5-20.1 for non-state candidates): contributions totaling at least $1,000 received and accepted not more than 25 days and not less than 48 hours before an election must be reported "not later than forty-eight (48) hours after the contribution is received and accepted."
- Computed 2027 dates (from the statute, NOT printed in the calendar, which says "Only selected dates after January 2027 are included"): municipal primary May 4, 2027 gives a report dated April 9, due noon April 16; municipal election Nov 2, 2027 gives a report dated Oct 8, due noon Oct 15. Weekend/holiday transfer rules were not checked. Mark as computed.
- Not read: 3-9-5-8, 8.2, 8.4, 8.5 (late entrants, petition candidates, write-ins, vacancy candidates), and the statement-of-organization threshold ($500 per Chalkbeat, secondary).
Confidence high for quoted text; computed dates medium.

- Signatures (CAN-34 instructions): "In a metropolitan school corporation, this petition must be signed by ten (10) registered voters residing in the same board member district as the nominee. (IC 20-23-7-8.1) In a community school corporation, this petition must be signed by ten (10) registered voters residing within the boundaries of the school corporation. (IC 20-23-4-29.1) Additional petition requirements apply in some school corporations." Each candidate files a CAN-12 Statement of Economic Interests with the petition; the petition goes to the county with the largest share of the corporation's population; the party line is a party, "Independent", or blank. IC 3-8-2.5-2(c) defers the count to IC 20-23 / IC 20-25 (those sections were not read directly; the form cites them). IC 3-8-2.5-2(b): signers must be registered at the address on the petition and qualified to vote for the candidate.

- 2028 dates: not published; by the statute the window is the day registration reopens after the 2028 primary through 30 days later (verify the primary date).
Confidence high. No filing fee is stated (Chalkbeat, secondary).

Source: Election Division 2026 Election Calendar, Election Administrators Edition, revised March 2026 (covers through Nov 2027; text extracted locally), read 2026-11-08. It states "Only selected dates after January 2027 are included."

- Campaign finance: annual report due noon Wed Jan 20, 2027 (CFA-4); regular party committees March 1, 2027.
