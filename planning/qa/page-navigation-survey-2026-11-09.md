# Page navigation survey, 2026-11-09

Read-only survey of all 60 pages in `planning/qa/page-inventory-2026-11-09.tsv`. Evidence only; no recommendations are final.

## Method and caveats

- Section structure (h2/h3, table captions, headers, row counts) was pulled from the built HTML in `site/st-joseph/` with a Python HTML parser. Generator code was read in `tools/site/pages/*.py` and `tools/site/civic/map.py`.
- "Screens" = rendered page height at 1280x800 divided by 800, measured with Playwright against localhost:8787. Phone widths will be longer. Values were measured for 40 pages directly. The other 19 are inferred from sibling pages with the same template; each is marked "(est.)" in the table.
- Table rows overstate pain. Most tables sit behind "Show table" `<details>` (budgets: 125 details; county: 34; none opened by default). The visible length is mostly charts, cards and prose. Screens is the better measure than rows.
- Every page has a TOC (`class="toc"`) and a "Reference" menu. Only the home page, `/places/` and `/schools/` (and `/map/`, `/money/budgets/`, and the 3 community pages with indicator maps) have a map.
- Boundaries the guide has now: county, cities/towns, townships, school corporations, Census 2020 voting districts (precincts). Being added by another agent: commissioner districts, county council A-I, South Bend and Mishawaka council districts, SBCSC board districts, Walkerton wards, Indiana House and Senate districts. `sources/boundaries/districts/` already holds `in-house-2021`, `in-senate-2021`, `mishawaka-council-2022` and `south-bend-council-2022`.
- `/map/` already has a "How areas voted" explorer. It covers 2020, 2022 and 2024 general elections by township, city/town and precinct, for federal, state, county, township and town races. It does not cover city races, school boards, questions or 2026. Precinct results (`precinct_results.csv`, 24,876 rows) feed only that map.

Legend for the table:

- Key = geographic key in the content.
- Map = would a map help (yes / partly / no).
- Effort S/M/L = rough size of the work.
- Conf = confidence in the verdict (H/M/L).

## 2. Pages with the most reader pain (in order)

1. `/data/` (159 screens, 3.3 MB). The 72-screen source list plus 14 truncated tables. It is a reference page, so fewer readers hit it, but it is the worst scroll and weight. Evidence: sections "Where the data comes from" 72 screens, "How to pull it yourself" 17.6.
2. `/money/campaigns/` (134 screens). In progress, not surveyed.
3. `/about/` (98.6 screens). 87 of them are the sources index.
4. `/money/budgets/` (92.6 screens, 151 tables). The map only covers 5 screens. Below it, "Budgets over time" is 26.4 screens of 79 unit series tables, with libraries/special units 16.2 and Debt 14.7.
5. `/elections/landscape/` (78.7 screens, 680 rows). "Margins in every race" is 41.5 screens of 353 rows with no office or place filter; "Current officeholders" is 13.3 screens (164 rows) and "Up next" is 7 screens (72 rows), both unfiltered.
6. `/schools/` (38.8 screens). Six corporations stacked, each with seat cards, enrollment, money and questions. The map exists but all sections still render.
7. `/history/` (31.5), `/county/` (31, with 33 sequential officeholder cards), `/elections/calendar/` (29.7, with 16 screens in the 189-row "Every seat" table), `/history/people/` (26.4).
8. `/state/your-legislators/` (22 screens). Nine districts' tables stacked; a reader must already know their district.
9. `/money/your-tax-bill/` (19.3). A reader has to know their 3-digit taxing district code; this is a "find my area" failure rather than a scroll failure.

## 3. Cross-cutting patterns

- **Tables keyed by district across many pages.** The same district keys recur: state House/Senate (your-legislators, landscape, map, calendar), commissioner and council districts (county, landscape, history, run/county-council), city council districts (South Bend, Mishawaka pages, landscape), school-board districts (schools, run/school-board), taxing districts (your-tax-bill, data). One district map per type could drive "your district" selectors on several pages.
- **Place pages share one template** (about 14 screens for townships and 12-13 for towns): At a glance, How organized, Who holds office, Party control, location, Recent results, Budget, State audits, Context, Gaps, Sources. They are not painful individually (each 9-14 screens, 50-60 rows). The cost is repeated boilerplate (Gaps and Sources are 5-9 screens on some pages). The two city pages differ: 19-21 screens because of council-district cards and 9 results tables.
- **Sources and Gaps sections** end every page and run 3-9 screens (county: 5.9; Mishawaka and SB: 5.7; landscape: 8.7; calendar: 8.1). They could be collapsed by default site-wide.
- **Collapsed tables are already the norm** ("Show table" details everywhere), so row counts mislead. Screens, not rows, drive pain. Remaining pain comes from stacked chart-plus-heading blocks (budgets: 79 series) and lists of cards (county, legislators, schools).
- **Filters already exist on** the results explorer (year/election/office/place), landscape margins (year/type/band/sort), campaigns (4 selects) and tax bill (district dropdown). They lack place/office/district filters.
- **No page offers a "my area" selection that persists into content.** The site has a "You are here / Change your place" bar (`here.js`), but pages like county, schools and landscape do not filter by it.
- **Precinct-level data (24,876 rows) feeds only /map/.** No other page uses precincts, and `run/precinct-committeeperson` has no precinct lookup.

## 4. Things I could not determine

- Mobile screen counts (only measured 1280 px). Mobile will be longer, roughly 1.5 to 2 times.
- Screens for 19 pages were estimated from sibling templates (marked "est."), not measured individually: 13 smaller place pages, 4 `run/` pages, `run/precinct-committeeperson` and `run/index`-adjacent ones.
- Whether New Prairie school-board district boundaries, library district boundaries, and county taxing-district boundaries are public. I did not search (no web searches allowed). The 36 taxing districts look derivable as intersections of existing township, city/town and school-corporation layers, but I did not verify against the DLGF district list.
- Whether the county GIS publishes parcel-level taxing district shapes (not checked).
- `/money/campaigns/` was not surveyed in depth by instruction.
- Exact visible row counts after JS filtering on landscape (the text said "353 races shown"; the table caption said 354 rows).
- Whether the results explorer's place list will accept new district layers without code changes (explorer data blob is built in `tools/site/pages/elections.py`, `_contest_payload`; not read in depth).
