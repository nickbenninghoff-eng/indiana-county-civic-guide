# Phase 6: the home page, led by the county map (2026-11-18)

Brief: redesign `index.html` so the county map is the way in (design direction, section 0, "maps organize the home
page"). Nothing is committed, pushed or deployed. No file under `data/` was edited and none of the forbidden build
tools was run.

## What the home page is now

Top to bottom (`tools/site/pages/landing.py`):

1. **What the guide is.** The county counted from the data in the display face ("One county. 37 local governments.
   173 elected seats.", unchanged rule and test), one sentence (who holds every seat, results since 2010 with the
   South Bend mayor back to 1963, the money, 9 state legislators, a "computed" mark), and a **large search box**. The
   box is a second copy of the header search: `static/js/search.js` now binds the same combobox to both boxes and loads
   `assets/search-index.json` once for both. "mayor money" in the home box lists "Mayor of South Bend › Money" first.
2. **Your place strip** (`#your-govs`). No saved place: one line and a "Choose your place" fold (`#pick`, the existing
   `place_picker`, still driven by `here.js`; Your place links to `index.html#pick`). With a saved place:
   "Your governments (saved on this device): [South Bend] [Portage Township] [SBCSC] [St. Joseph County]" as buttons
   that select each one on the map, and the map opens with the city or town (or the township) selected. Only what the
   reader chose counts (`civicHere.mine()`): with defaults only it says "Township and school corporation not chosen."
   and the fold reads "Choose your township and school corporation, or change your place". A place picked in the fold
   updates the strip and the map at once (`civic:place`). A link to Your place sits at the right of the strip.
3. **The map** (`#map`, `#home-map`). Nine layers in two labelled groups:
   Governments (County, Cities and towns, Townships, School corporations) and Election districts (County
   commissioners, County council, City councils, Indiana House, Indiana Senate). It reuses the Offices hub's model
   (`pages/offices.py hub_areas`, imported, unchanged) and `civic/map.py` / `civic/district_map.py` helpers; every
   path is one of the boundary files' paths (a test checks this). Fills tell kinds or neighbours apart only.
   - **Click (or Enter) selects** an area and opens its links in the panel; a second click on the same area, or the
     panel's button, opens its page. Keyboard: Enter moves focus to the panel heading, Tab goes through its links,
     Escape returns to the area. JS off: every area is a plain link to its government or office page.
   - **Government panel:** Its page (Overview · Elections · Money · History, only tabs that exist), every office it
     elects with its current holders (party tag, name linked to the person page by the existing `link_names`), buttons
     to the layer that draws its district seats, a note for seats with no boundary file, and the next election year.
     The county panel lists the countywide offices, one line for the courts (10 judgeships, linked to the county page's
     `#courts`) and buttons to the commissioner and council layers.
   - **District panel:** held by (person link), the office page and its tabs (Overview · Elections · Money · History),
     the last election it was on (the election page), the next election, and the government it belongs to. The
     district set's map note (which official map it is) shows under the map while that layer is on.
4. **Five doors** (`#doors`): Governments, Offices, Elections, Money, Learn, each one line and up to three deep links
   chosen from the data, each only when its page is built: City of South Bend › Money, Centre Township, South Bend
   Community School Corporation › Elections; Mayor of South Bend › Money, County Sheriff › Elections, People; 2026
   general election, 2026 primary election, Close races; Budgets compared, Your tax bill, Campaign money; Learning
   paths, How elections work, Who handles this?
5. **Next on the ballot** (`#next-ballot`): the Elections hub's own line (`pages/elections.py _hub_next`, imported):
   2027, primary and general dates, the governments with seats on it, links to the hub's `#next` and the calendar.
6. **Every government and district, as a list** (`#list`): the no-JavaScript form of the map: governments by type with
   their tab links, and every drawn district by layer. It ships open; `folds.js` closes it with JS on.
7. Sources (the usual fold), footer.

## Follow-up (2026-11-18, the owner's answers and the coordinator's review)

### 1. The headline count: one number, worded so it is true

The two numbers counted different things:
- 37 was units.csv minus the two state chambers. That includes the court system and the board of health (both part of the county government) and the two housing authorities (no page). It leaves out two libraries and five special units that do have pages.
- 40 is the government pages (`civic/governments.py governments()`).

Neither is defensible as "40 local governments". Two of the 40 are not separate units of government, going by the guide's own data:
- **The South Bend Redevelopment Commission.** `appointed_boards.csv` lists it as a board of the city, not a unit. The city's monthly report (MON-077) carries it as "Redevelopment Commission controlled funds".
- **The Olive-New Carlisle-Hudson Fire Protection Territory.** Its funds appear inside the townships' Gateway budgets ("SPECIAL FIRE PROTECTION TERRITORY GENERAL").

**Decision:** count the pages and word it "**40 governments and public bodies**". A line under the sentence says exactly what is counted: "Counted: the county, 2 cities, 7 towns, 13 townships, 6 school corporations, 4 public libraries and 7 special units, each with its own page in the guide."

**Implementation:**
- One function, `civic/governments.py count()` (added; nothing re-signed), gives the number, the noun and the breakdown.
- It is used by the headline, the home list's summary ("40 governments and public bodies, each with its page") and the Governments hub's facts line ("40 governments and public bodies in St. Joseph County have a page in the guide: ..."). The hub line is a small edit in `pages/governments.py`.
- `test_phase6_home.test_one_count_everywhere` asserts all three agree and that the courts, the board of health, the housing authorities and the state chambers are not counted. `test_landing_header` now checks the new wording.

**In (40):**
- the county
- 2 cities: South Bend, Mishawaka
- 7 towns: Indian Village, Lakeville, New Carlisle, North Liberty, Osceola, Roseland, Walkerton
- 13 townships
- 6 school corporations: SBCSC, PHM, School City of Mishawaka, John Glenn, LaVille (Union-North), New Prairie
- 4 public libraries: St. Joseph County, Mishawaka-Penn-Harris, New Carlisle, Walkerton
- 7 special units:
  - Transpo
  - the Airport Authority
  - the Regional Water and Sewer District
  - the Solid Waste Management District
  - the St. Joseph Redevelopment Authority (legal form not verified in the guide's data)
  - the South Bend Redevelopment Commission (a city board)
  - the Olive-New Carlisle-Hudson Fire Protection Territory

**Out:**
- the county courts and the board of health: part of the county government, with no page of their own
- the Housing Authority of South Bend and the Mishawaka housing authority: no page
- the Indiana House and Senate

**Caveat:** "public body" fits the commission and the authorities. It is loosest for the fire protection territory, which is administered by the units in it. If the owner wants the strictest count, dropping the territory and the commission would give 38 "governments and public bodies". That would mean leaving pages out of the count, so the hub and the home list would no longer match it without a second rule.

The "173 elected seats" line is unchanged: offices of every unit except the state chambers, judges included.

- The panel now shows the hint and then the shown layer's areas as a list, each a link to its page, for example "CITIES AND TOWNS ON THE MAP (9)". Lists are alphabetical, with numbers in numeric order (`_natural`), and every area gets the same treatment.

- With JS on, choosing a list item selects the area like the map does (Enter focuses the panel heading). With JS off the items are plain links.
- `map.js` puts back the block it saw at load whenever no area is chosen. `home.js` then shows the list for the layer that is on (`syncStart`).
- New test: `test_panel_start_state_lists_the_layer_areas`. It checks:
  - one list per layer, and only the default list shown

  - natural order
  - no "open" button
