# City of South Bend, Indiana: research notes (collected 2026-11-07)

Collect-and-report only. Confidence tags: H = official/primary or multiple agreeing sources; M = single reasonably reliable secondary source; L = conflicting, partial, or unverified.
Wikipedia raw wikitext was pulled for the mayoral-elections page; its figures cite Indiana Election Division / newspapers, but I did not independently check each citation.

## 1. Structure

### Election cycle (confirmed)
- Mayor, Common Council and City Clerk are elected in odd-numbered years, the year before a presidential election. Last: Nov 7, 2023 (primary May 2, 2023). Next: 2027 (Wikipedia infobox "next_year = 2027"; Tribune/Wikipedia text: "every four years in the year immediately preceding ... presidential elections"). Partisan primaries are held in May (2019: May 7; 2023: May 2). Confidence H.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayoral_elections_in_South_Bend,_Indiana
  - https://www.953mnc.com/2019/05/07/indiana-primary-and-special-election-results/
  - https://www.wvpe.org/wvpe-news/2023-05-02/2023-election-results
- Mayor term: 4 years, beginning noon Jan 1 after the election; must live in the city (IC 36-4-5-2, via FindLaw): https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-36-local-government/in-code-sect-36-4-5-2/
- City Clerk in second class cities: elected by voters, 4-year term beginning noon Jan 1 (IC 36-4-10-2, via FindLaw): https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-36-local-government/in-code-sect-36-4-10-2/

### City class
- NOT directly confirmed. No fetched source states "South Bend is a second class city". Indirect evidence: the council structure (6 districts + 3 at-large) is the structure IC 36-4-6-3 prescribes for second class cities, and Wikipedia's infobox gives "Strong Mayor-Council". Confidence L on the class label itself (statutory class is by population; not looked up).
  - https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-36-local-government/in-code-sect-36-4-6-3/
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend,_Indiana

### Mayor (executive)
- Elected citywide; the city's executive under Indiana law (IC 36-4-5-2). Mayor can veto ordinances; Wikipedia says a six-vote majority of the council overrides (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend_Common_Council).
- Under IC 36-4-9-6 (second class cities) the executive appoints a controller, civil engineer, corporation counsel, fire chief, police chief and other officers (secondary summary of statute; text truncated, verify): https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-36-local-government/in-code-sect-36-4-9-6/ . Confidence M.
- Board of Public Works and Safety: IC 36-4-9-5 establishes one such board per city; a second class city's council may by ordinance split it into separate works and safety boards (older code text, verify current): https://legalfix.com/statutes/state-codes/indiana/title-36/article-4/chapter-9/section-36-4-9-5 . South Bend's actual board setup not searched.
- Redevelopment Commission: per a South Bend city page cited in search, five voting members, three appointed by the Mayor and two by the Common Council, plus a non-voting school board adviser appointed by the mayor (page snippet only, not fully fetched): https://southbendin.gov/?p=139649 . Confidence M.
- Current police chief and public-safety structure: not searched.

### Common Council (legislative)
- 9 members: 6 district seats + 3 at-large; 4-year terms; unicameral. Each voter casts one district vote and up to three at-large votes (IC 36-4-6-3 summary). Districts must be contiguous, compact, roughly equal population, redrawn only at times allowed by IC 3-5-10. Confidence H.
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend_Common_Council
  - https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-36-local-government/in-code-sect-36-4-6-3/
- Powers (city site): resolutions (internal affairs) and ordinances (Municipal Code, fees, penalties, budgets, salaries, zoning changes). Meets 2nd and 4th Mondays; agenda items filed with the City Clerk by noon the Wednesday before. https://southbendin.gov/government/common-council/
- Council first elected June 5, 1865 (city incorporated May 22, 1865) per Wikipedia citing Howard's history of St. Joseph County: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend_Common_Council . Confidence M.
- Budget approval: ordinances "including ... budgets" per the city page above. Detailed budget process (state DLGF review etc.) not researched.

### City Clerk
- Elected citywide. Office is "administrative backbone of the Common Council": prepares agendas, keeps council records, reads bills, takes roll call votes, oversees ordinance and parking ticket collection. https://southbendin.gov/government/city-clerk/ ; https://www.abc57.com/news/dawn-jones-elected-as-south-bend-city-clerk-in-special-caucus-vote
- No other elected city offices were identified (no separate elected treasurer/controller; controller is appointed per statute summary). Confidence M.

### Vacancy filling (documented cases)
- Statute: IC 3-13-11 (caucus chaired by the county party chair of the same party as the departing official, IC 3-13-11-6 summary only). I could not fetch the sections that say when a caucus is used for mayor/council/clerk. Confidence L on statute detail; practice below is documented.
  - https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-3-elections/in-code-sect-3-13-11-6/
- Council (practice, 2026): 4th District Democratic precinct chairs (7 voters, secret ballot) chose Heidi Beidinger on May 20, 2026 over Hodge Patel, Derrick Perry, Angela Smith to replace Troy Warner, who resigned to be Mayor Mueller's chief of staff. https://www.wvpe.org/wvpe-news/2026-05-20/beidinger-wins-warners-south-bend-council-seat-in-party-caucus . Term expected to end Dec 31, 2027 per search summary of other coverage (https://www.wndu.com/2026/05/20/heidi-beidinger-burnett-chosen-take-vacant-south-bend-common-council-seat/). WNDU/Notre Dame Observer use "Beidinger-Burnett"; WVPE/Wikipedia use "Beidinger".
- Clerk (practice, 2019): Democratic district precinct chairs voted in a special caucus Aug 15, 2019 (seven ballots, lowest eliminated each round); Dawn Jones beat Lisa Plencer on the final ballot to replace Kareemah Fowler (resigned July 2019 to work for South Bend schools). https://www.abc57.com/news/dawn-jones-elected-as-south-bend-city-clerk-in-special-caucus-vote
- Mayor (practice, 1997): Council "appointed unanimously" Steve Luecke to fill the vacancy when Joe Kernan became Lt. Governor (Wikipedia, Luecke page; no mention of caucus there). Wikipedia infobox says Luecke served to Jan 1, 2012; body text says "until 2011" (conflict). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Luecke . Confidence M; mechanism (council vs. party caucus) not confirmed by a second source.

## 5. Turning points (neutral, sourced)
- 1865: City incorporated May 22, 1865; first mayor William G. George (Republican) elected that year; first council elected June 5, 1865. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayoral_elections_in_South_Bend,_Indiana ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend_Common_Council
- Sep 2, 1960: Mayor Edward Voorde died in office (reported as an automobile accident); Frank Bruggner took over as acting mayor in Sept 1960 and did not seek election in 1963. How Bruggner was selected (council, caucus, succession rule) was NOT confirmed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_South_Bend,_Indiana ; search summary of city docs https://docs.southbendin.gov/WebLink/0/doc/42024/Page7.aspx
- 1963 and 1967: Republican Lloyd Allen elected, then re-elected with 58% (his margin about 10,500). 1971: Democrat Jerry Miller won; begins continuous Democratic control of the mayor's office. Miller campaigned on a cabinet form of government and restructured the executive branch after taking office. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayoral_elections_in_South_Bend,_Indiana
- 1971: Janet Allen (R), the first woman elected to the Common Council (1963), was the Republican nominee. 1991: Sylvia Shelton (R) also lost. No woman has been elected mayor (Wikipedia, as of 2025).
- 1979: Lowest mayoral turnout since 1925 (29,196 votes). 1987: closest modern general election (Kernan 53%-47%).
- Jan 3, 1997: Mayor Joe Kernan resigned after being elected Lt. Governor (1996); Steve Luecke, a council member since 1988, was appointed unanimously by the council per Wikipedia (single source), then won 1999, 2003, 2007. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Luecke
- 2011-2012: Open seat; Pete Buttigieg won the primary with 54.9% and general with 73.85%. Took office Jan 1, 2012. Resigned/term ended Jan 1, 2020 (per list) after 2019 election of James Mueller.
- 2019: Clerk Kareemah Fowler resigned in July 2019 after the filing/withdrawal deadlines; she remained on the Nov ballot with no Republican opponent; Dawn Jones was chosen by Democratic precinct-chair caucus (Aug 15, 2019). ABC57 link above.
- 2023: Democrats won all 9 council seats, flipping District 5 (51-49, 37 votes) from an R incumbent in a district that had elected Republicans since at least the early 1990s (WVPE). Council described as "female majority" (ABC57).
- May 2026: Council seat vacancy filled by caucus (see section 1).
