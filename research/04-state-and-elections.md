## C. How the General Assembly works

Source: [W-GA] and general knowledge; iga.in.gov not fetched. [M]

- Convenes first Tuesday after the first Monday in January.
- **Long session** (odd years): 61 legislative days, must adjourn by April 30. **Short session** (even years): 30 days, adjourn by March 15. Cannot adjourn more than 3 days without both houses' consent. Governor can call a special session.
- House: 100 members, 2-year terms. Senate: 50 members, 4-year terms, staggered (about half up every two years). No term limits.
- Veto override: simple majority of members in each chamber (per Wikipedia; Indiana is among the few states with a low override threshold). [M]
- Quorum: Wikipedia excerpt did not state it. NOT FOUND. (Indiana Constitution art. 4 sec. 10: two-thirds of each house; this is general knowledge, [L] verify - relevant because of walkouts.)
- How a bill becomes law: not collected from an official source. Standard path to verify at iga.in.gov: introduction, committee, 2nd and 3rd reading, other chamber, conference/concurrence, governor 7 days to sign/veto. [L - not sourced]

## D. State-local relationship

- **Home Rule Act (IC 36-1-3, 1980)**: abrogated Dillon's Rule; units have all powers needed for effective local government; doubts resolved in favor of the power; limits include no power to regulate conduct regulated by a state agency except as granted by statute. Cedar Lake v. Alessia (Ind. Ct. App. 2013) held trial court erred by applying Dillon's Rule. [H] [Justia IC 36-1-3](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/2011/title36/article1/chapter3/), [Case Clips](https://caseclips.courts.in.gov/2013/03/21/cedar-lake-v-alessia). Applicability to townships: older law-review note says units "except townships" - current code says all units; verify.
- **Property tax caps** (constitutional "circuit breaker"; 1%/2%/3% of assessed value for homesteads / rental and agricultural / other): NOT retrieved from a source in this run (Wikipedia page 404). [L - from general knowledge; source before publishing]
- **SEA 1 (2025)**, signed April 2025. Reported provisions (sources disagree on some schedule details; check enrolled text): [AIM summary](https://aimindiana.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Mini-Quick-Guide-SEA-1-Property-Tax-Deductions-and-Credits.pdf), [Purdue Extension](https://extension.purdue.edu/news/2025/09/property-tax-reform-phasing-out-phasing-in.html), [Capital Chronicle](https://indianacapitalchronicle.com/2025/11/17/analysis-property-tax-changes-to-put-more-pressure-on-businesses-owners-of-low-value-homes/)
  - Standard homestead deduction phased down (one guide: $40,000 in 2026, $30,000 2027, $20,000 2028, $10,000 2029, eliminated 2030) while supplemental deduction rises (to 66.7% by 2031). Journal Gazette gives different timing.
  - New 10% homestead credit up to $300 starting 2026 (+$150 for qualifying low-income seniors).
  - Business personal property exemption raised from $80,000 to $2 million.
  - **Local income tax:** LIT councils eliminated starting July 1, 2027, county fiscal body becomes the adopting body; from 2028 maximum LIT expenditure rate rises to 2.9% and cities/towns may impose a municipal LIT; LIT property-tax-relief credits eliminated in 2028.
  - Estimated statewide lost local revenue $1.4 billion over 2026-2028; Speaker Huston said lawmakers "will probably have some tweaks." 2026 follow-up: HEA 1210 expands disabled veteran relief. [M]
- **DLGF**: site shows budget data (Gateway), additional appropriations, debt, local income tax, referendums, TIF, abstract, appeal forms (taxpayers appeal to county first). Precise statutory budget-approval authority text NOT retrieved. [L]
- **School funding**: state "tuition support" is the largest budget line; formula = per-student foundation grant (by enrollment/ADM) plus complexity grants (low-income) plus special education, career-tech, academic performance and other grants; curricular materials (about $158/student) moved into the formula for 2025-26; appropriated about $9.4B FY2026 and $9.7B FY2027 (one source $9.6B for 2026-27). Referendum (local operating levy) mechanics NOT found. [M] [Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/indiana/2025/02/20/bills-affecting-education-funding-pass/), [WFYI](https://www.wfyi.org/news/articles/indiana-2026-2027-state-budget-funding-for-marion-county-schools)

## E. Indiana election law and mechanics

### E1. Calendar
- General Election: first Tuesday after first Monday in November [H W-Elections]. 2026: Nov 3. Primary 2026: May 5 (early voting began April 7 per search results; St. Joseph County early voting sites April 25-May 4). [M]
- Even years: federal, state, legislators, county and township and school board (school board per CAN forms listing). Odd years: municipal (CAN-44 "city or town office (2027)"). Mayor elections 2023, 2027. [M]
- Statewide offices other than governor and AG are on ballot in presidential-midterm patterns; 2026 had SOS, Treasurer, Auditor/Comptroller. [H]
- Registration deadline: General 2026 = Oct 5, 2026; primary April 6, 2026 (29 days before) [H] [SOS](https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/voter-information/register-to-vote/). Eligibility: US citizen, Indiana resident, 18 by next general or municipal election, not imprisoned after conviction, lived in precinct 30 days.

### E2. Primaries and party affiliation
- Partisan primaries; no party registration. Voter picks one party's ballot at the polls and that choice is recorded in public record. Ballotpedia describes Indiana as closed by law while officials call it "semi-open." Poll-worker challenge standard: voted majority of that party's candidates last general or intends to in upcoming election. Candidate rule: two most recent primary votes must match the party they run under. [M] [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Primary_elections_in_Indiana)
- 2025 proposals (Sen. Gaskill closed-primary bill; SB 201 declaration + blackout window): **Senate killed them** (also cut-early-voting bills). No law change found for 2026. [M] [Inside Indiana Business](https://www.insideindianabusiness.com/?p=182194)

### E3. Ballot access
- Primary candidates: Declaration of Candidacy (CAN-2); precinct committee/delegate (CAN-37). [H] [SOS candidate forms](https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/candidate-information/)
- Independents and minor parties: Petition of Nomination (CAN-19 state/legislative; CAN-21 local; CAN-34 school board); petition filed with the county voter registration office by noon June 30, 2026 for signature verification; consent form (CAN-20) due noon July 15, 2026. For Secretary of State 2026, independent Greg Ballard needed 36,943 valid signatures and reported submitting 52,000+ (basis percentage, filing outcome unconfirmed; he appears on ballot per Wikipedia as "Lincoln Party"). [M] [Capital Chronicle](https://indianacapitalchronicle.com/2026/06/20/independent-secretary-of-state-hopeful-says-he-has-the-signatures/)
- Primary filing deadlines and where to file: not retrieved (2026 candidate guide PDF 404). Expect county clerk/voter registration for local; Election Division for state. [L]
- Libertarian: state chair certifies ballot vacancies (CAN-32, CAN-33). Major-party nominees for SOS, Treasurer, Auditor chosen at **convention**, not primary (per Wikipedia).

### E4. Vacancies and party structure
- Legislative vacancy: caucus of precinct committeepersons of the vacating member's party in the district. County/local vacancies: caucus of eligible precinct committeepersons or county chair (IC 3-13-11 text partly retrieved). Candidates file declaration with county chair at least 72 hours before caucus. Elected precinct committeepersons are eligible regardless of vacancy date; appointed ones generally must have served 30 days. [M] [FindLaw IC 3-13-11-5](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-3-elections/in-code-sect-3-13-11-5/), [IC 3-13-1-10](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/indiana/statute/3-13-1-10)
- Examples: House 42 (Parke Co.) GOP caucus Jan 14, 2025 [indiana.gop](https://www.indiana.gop/news/advisory-house-district-42-caucus-set-january-14th); House 9 Randy Novak (D) appointed 2025 by caucus (inferred from "initially appointed"; method not confirmed). St. Joseph County examples: Mishawaka councilman Tony Hazen sought to fill Commissioner Deb Fleming's term (date and method not confirmed; Hazen is now a commissioner per Wikipedia). [L]
- Precinct committeepersons are elected in the primary (CAN-37) every four years; county chairs elected by committeepersons. [L - not sourced here]

### E5. Voting mechanics
- Early (in-person absentee): 28 days before election, ends noon the day before; all counties must offer the two Saturdays before. [H] [SOS absentee](https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/voter-information/ways-to-vote/absentee-voting/)
- Mail absentee: needs one of 11 qualifying reasons (e.g., 65+, disability, away all 12 hours, election worker); application received by 11:59 pm 12 days before; ballot received by 6 pm Election Day; postmarks do not count. Since July 1, 2023, application needs photo ID copy or ID number (driver's license/state ID, voter ID, or last four SSN). [H]
- Photo ID at polls: SOS page 404; Indiana requires state/federal photo ID at the polls [M, search snippet: "bring a state of Indiana or federal issued photo ID"].
- St. Joseph County: vote centers used (approx. 41 in Nov 2024; 42 reported for a primary); Election Board approved a vote-center plan and satellite absentee offices (County-City Building South Bend; County Service Building Mishawaka) on Feb 25, 2026. Third early site (Centre Township library) added 2024. [M] [WNDU](https://www.wndu.com/2026/04/07/early-voting-begins-indiana-primary-election-st-joseph-county/), [ABC57](https://www.abc57.com/news/st-joseph-county-adding-two-new-voting-centers)
- Straight-ticket voting: **not found**. (Indiana eliminated straight-ticket voting in 2019 - [L, general knowledge], needs source.)
- Citizen initiatives/referenda: **not found**. Indiana has no statewide citizen initiative or referendum; local school operating referenda exist (levy, voter-approved). [L]
