## 2. Indiana statutes (Justia unless noted)

Caution: Justia's "2025 Indiana Code" may not reflect 2025-2026 amendments (for example the 2025 property-tax overhaul). Verify against https://iga.in.gov/laws before publication.

**Property tax caps - IC 6-1.1-20.6-7.5.** https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-6/article-1-1/chapter-20-6/section-6-1-1-20-6-7-5/ (chapter index: https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-6/article-1-1/chapter-20-6/). Plain language: for taxes due after 2009, a taxpayer gets a credit for the amount by which tax on their property exceeds a percent of gross assessed value: 1% for homesteads; 2% for other residential property, long-term care property and agricultural land; 3% for nonresidential real property and personal property. Taxes approved by voters in a referendum, and certain debt-service and lease payments on pre-July 2008 bonds, are excluded from the calculation (7.5(b)-(c)). Under section 8 (https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-6/article-1-1/chapter-20-6/section-6-1-1-20-6-8/) a person generally need not apply; the county auditor identifies the property and applies the credit. The revenue lost to credits falls on local units, not the state. Adopted in P.L.146-2008 (7.5 as added by that act).

**Const. Art. 10 sec. 1.** https://law.justia.com/constitution/indiana/art10.html - Text retrieved: (a) General Assembly must provide a uniform and equal rate of property assessment and taxation and a just valuation; may exempt listed classes (municipal/educational/religious/charitable property, tangible and intangible personal property not held as investment, and a principal residence); (b) may exempt motor vehicles, mobile homes, airplanes, boats, trailers etc. if an excise tax is substituted. Amendment history on that page: 1966, 2004. IMPORTANT: the Justia text shows NO percentage cap language, although Indiana voters approved a property tax cap amendment in Nov 2010 (Public Question 1). Either Justia is stale or the 2010 text sits elsewhere. Needs verification on iga.in.gov/Ballotpedia; the 1%/2%/3% figures above are statutory.

**Caucus - legislative vacancies, IC 3-13-5.** Index: https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-3/article-13/chapter-5/ ; sec. 1: https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-3/article-13/chapter-5/section-3-13-5-1/ . Plain language: a vacancy in the Indiana House or Senate is filled by a caucus of the precinct committeemen from that district who belong to the same party that elected the departed member. The caucus meets within 30 days of the vacancy (a state chair may call it early if a resignation, election to other office, or similar is pending) and picks a successor by majority vote; proxies by vice committeemen are allowed (sec. 5). Per sec. 2 the STATE chairman of that party sets the place and time and mails notice to committeemen at least 10 days before. The Secretary of State certifies the person selected (sec. 7). Note: Ballotpedia's description says the state committee appoints for an independent-held seat via special election; see 3-13-5-0.1.

**Caucus - local vacancies, IC 3-13-11.** Index: https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-3/article-13/chapter-11/ ; secs. 1, 3, 5. Plain language: when a local office (and certain school board seats) is vacated and a statute calls for a caucus, within 10 days the county chairman of the vacating officeholder's party (from the county holding the largest share of the office's election district population) gives notice of a caucus (sec. 3). Eligible caucus members are precinct committeemen of that same party in precincts whose voters could vote for the departing official; appointed committeemen qualify if they held the post 30 days before the vacancy (sec. 5). Selection is by secret ballot (sec. 10); results are certified and filed (sec. 11). The pick is a "pro tempore" appointment under Const. Art. 2 sec. 11. The statute is triggered by IC 3-13-6-2, 3-13-6-3(a), 3-13-7-1, 3-13-8-1, 3-13-9-1, 3-13-10-1 and IC 20-23-4-30 (sec. 1). Sections beyond those read (e.g. 8, 9) not read in detail.

**County council and commissioners - IC 36-2-3 and IC 36-2-2.** https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-36/article-2/chapter-3/ and .../chapter-2/ (4 text: sections 36-2-3-2, 36-2-3-4; 36-2-2-4). KEY FINDING: IC 36-2-2-4(c) and IC 36-2-3-4 create an opt-in structure for "a county having a population of more than 185,000 and less than 300,000 that opts in ... by passing a resolution by a majority vote of its executive body not later than September 1, 2023." Opted-in counties: three single-member commissioner districts (36-2-2-4(c)); county council of four single-member district seats plus three at-large seats (36-2-3-4(a)). If the county did not opt in by 9/1/2023 it is governed by the general provisions of 36-2-2. The statute text does not name St. Joseph County, but its population (about 272,000) falls in the range. WHETHER St. Joseph County opted in is NOT verified here. The St. Joseph County council has traditionally been 9 members (not checked here). Check with the county's commissioners' 2023 minutes.

**Term limits - Const. Art. 6 sec. 2.** https://law.justia.com/constitution/indiana/art6.html (also https://secure.in.gov/history/.../article-6-administrative/ ). Via WebSearch summary: no person may serve as county Clerk, Auditor, Recorder, Treasurer, Sheriff or Coroner for more than 8 years in any 12-year period; Surveyor not on the list; terms are 4 years; pro tempore service (Art. 2 sec. 11) does not count. Amended 1952, 1984, 2004. Statutory tie-in IC 36-1-8-15. I did not read the constitution text itself in the browser (search summary only).

**Township government - IC 36-6.** Chapter index only (IC 36-6-1): https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-36/article-6/chapter-1/ . Not summarized (text not read; see Gaps).

**City classes - IC 36-4-1-1.** https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-36/article-4/chapter-1/section-36-4-1-1/ . Text: municipalities are classified by population: cities of 600,000 or more are first class; cities of 34,000 to 599,999 are second class; cities under 34,000 are third class; "other municipalities of any population" are towns. Subsection (b) says a city that reaches 34,000 stays second class (the rest of (b)/(c) not read). South Bend (about 103,000) and Mishawaka (about 50,000) therefore fall in the population band of second class; the classification of each is NOT separately verified against a city source.

**Straight-ticket voting.** IC 3-11-2-12 is the general-election ballot ORDER statute (offices listed in order after public questions), not the straight-ticket rule itself (https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-3/article-11/chapter-2/section-3-11-2-12/). Straight-party device rules live elsewhere in IC 3-11-2 (e.g. 3-11-2-6, 3-11-2-10) - not read. From search: NCSL notes SB 61 (2016) removed straight-party voting for at-large races; the Indiana Secretary of State's 2026 election legislation summary (https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/files/2026-Indiana-Election-Legislation-Summary.FINAL.pdf) lists HEA 1377 (2026), effective Jan 1, 2027, saying straight-party votes will not count for school board candidates. Whether straight-ticket voting is otherwise still in effect for 2026 is NOT confirmed. Needs the SOS PDF.

**Photo ID - IC 3-5-2-40.5.** https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-3/article-5/chapter-2/section-3-5-2-40-5/ . Defines "proof of identification" as a document that (1) shows the name of the individual it was issued to, conforming to the voter registration record, and (continues, not fully read) - first lines read only. Voting-place photo ID requirement itself is in IC 3-11-8-25.1 (not read). Summary from knowledge, unverified: a government-issued photo ID with an expiration date that is current or expired after the last general election.
