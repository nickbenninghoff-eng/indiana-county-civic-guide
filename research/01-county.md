## 1. Structure of county government

### Division of power (Indiana law)
- Indiana Code 36-2-2: three-member Board of County Commissioners = county executive. IC 36-2-3: county council = county fiscal body. (Source: IC 36-2-3-2 via FindLaw; IN county brochure.)
- Generally, in most Indiana counties: council has fiscal jurisdiction (approves/fixes annual budgets, sets salaries, sets tax rates/levies, authorizes bonds/borrowing, appropriates funds); commissioners handle regulatory/administrative matters (county property, roads/bridges, contracts/bids, appointments to boards, administering elections with election board).
- **St. Joseph County special case:** Per the Indiana county-government brochure (in.gov/counties/wabash), "In all but three of the counties: Lake, Marion, and St. Joseph, the legislative power of the county is divided between the board of county commissioners and the county council. In these three counties full legislative power is vested in the council by state statute." Council is the home-rule custodian and can grant commissioners additional powers (IC 36-2-3.5 context). Confidence: medium-high (state brochure, not the statute text; statute pages returned 403).
- St. Joseph is one of only three Indiana counties (with Lake and Marion) where commissioners are elected by district rather than at-large (ABC57, Apr 22, 2026). The district system dates to the 1970s (ABC57).

### Board of Commissioners
- 3 members, one per district (Districts 1, 2, 3), each elected only by voters in that district; 4-year terms; partisan elections. Staggered: District 1 elected 2022 and 2026; Districts 2 and 3 elected 2020 and 2024 (inferred from Wikipedia/WVPE/ABC57; medium confidence).
- Statutory (not constitutional) office; no term limit (brochure: commissioners, council, assessor, surveyor are exempt from the 8-in-12 limit).
- **Court ruling (April 2026):** ABC57 (Apr 22, 2026) reported a judge ruled that St. Joseph County's method of electing commissioners violates the Indiana Constitution's "free and equal elections" guarantee, because each voter helps elect only one of three commissioners. Remedy could be a switch to at-large voting in a future cycle; no change for the 2026 primaries; appeal possible. Judge, court, case name, date of ruling, and appeal status: NOT stated in the article; not found. The November 2026 election was held under the district system (District 1 voted only by District 1). Wikipedia notes the ruling and says future status is uncertain. Prior related litigation: City of South Bend sued the state over the district-election law (cited in search results; not fetched).
- Commissioner vacancy: filled by party caucus (see Appointed/caucus below).

### County Council
- 9 members, each from one of 9 single-member districts (A through I); 4-year terms; partisan. 9-member size applies to counties with population 185,000-300,000 that opt into the IC 36-2-2-4(c) structure (IC 36-2-3-2(b), FindLaw). Other Indiana counties have 7.
- Staggered: Districts A, B, C, E, H up in 2022 and 2026; Districts D, F, G, I up in 2024 (derived from 2022/2024/2026 results below).
- Fiscal body (see above); council also holds full legislative power in St. Joseph County.
- Redistricting history: Republican-majority commissioners passed maps in 2021 giving them a 2-1 advantage and making several council seats more competitive; Democrats (then the council majority) sued; Council overrode commissioners' veto of a Democratic plan (WVPE, Dec 30, 2021); a mediated settlement in early 2022 produced compromise maps (WVPE, Nov 9, 2022).

## 2. Courts

- **Circuit Court:** elected judge, 6-year term (brochure; Art. 7). Current: John Broden (D) per Wikipedia. Date elected not verified. (The 2024 general had a Probate judge race below.)
- **Probate Court:** Loris Zappia (R) defeated incumbent Jason Cichowicz (D), 54%-46% in Nov 2024 (WVPE, Nov 6, 2024). Probate judge is elected in partisan race.
- **Superior Court:** merit selection, not partisan elections. Per IC 33-33-71 (via search snippets of Justia/FindLaw/Indiana Lawyer): a local judicial nominating commission screens candidates and sends the governor the five most highly qualified; the governor appoints; judges serve successive 6-year terms and face a yes/no retention vote at the general election just before the term expires (IC 33-33-71-43). Nominees must be St. Joseph County domiciled, US citizens, Indiana-licensed lawyers; party affiliation may not be considered. St. Joseph is one of two Indiana counties using this method for Superior Court (the other is Allen County, per general knowledge; the search snippet said "two counties" without naming the other). A 2010-era Indiana Lawyer item mentions a legislative effort to move St. Joseph Superior Court to partisan elections ("House passes St. Joseph judicial election bill") -- outcome not confirmed here.
- **Number of Superior Court judges:** Wikipedia says eight; Indiana Lawyer items c. 2010 also say eight. Current count not confirmed from official source (not searched further).
- Retention results for 2026 not found (not stated in election-night articles fetched).
- Sources: https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/2018/title-33/article-33/chapter-71/section-33-33-71-43/ (snippet only), https://stjoebar.org/for-everyone/judicial-survey (bar survey of judges up for retention, snippet only).

## 6. Notable events and turning points (neutral)
- 2021: Republican-majority commissioners adopted new district maps over the Democratic-majority council's plan; council voted to override the commissioners' veto (WVPE, Dec 30, 2021); Democrats sued; a mediated settlement with compromise maps was reached early 2022 and approved by an Elkhart County judge (cost: "hundreds of thousands of dollars" in legal fees per WVPE).
- 2022: Republicans won a 5-4 council majority; WNDU described it as the first Republican council majority. Commissioners all Republican in 2020-2022.
- Aug 2022: GOP caucus picked Carl Baxmeyer to replace resigning commissioner Andy Kostielney; Baxmeyer elected board president 2-1 (Dieter dissenting); Dieter criticized the selection as party politics (WVPE).
- Jan 2025: Council Republicans disagreed over leadership; Democrat Bryan Tanner chosen president with Republican votes; Jan 2026: Tanner re-elected 6-3 with 2 of 5 Republican votes (953mnc, ABC57).
- Oct 2022: commissioners approved an investigation of elections (WVPE reference only).
- Apr 22, 2026: judge ruled the county's commissioner election system violates the "free and equal elections" clause (ABC57). Appeal status unknown.
- Nov 2026 (unofficial): Democrats won all contested county offices on the ballot (sheriff, prosecutor, commissioner D1, clerk, auditor, assessor, 3 council seats); Republican county leaders conceded (WNDU headline Nov 4, 2026).
- Jan 2026: Prosecutor Cotter announced retirement; Sheriff Redman term-limited, ran for commissioner.
