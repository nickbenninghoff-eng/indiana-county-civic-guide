## PART A - Notable people and political families

Criteria key: TENURE long tenure; FIRST; HIGHER later higher office; ACCOMP documented accomplishments/turning points; FAMILY political family.

### A1. Schuyler Colfax (1823-1885) and Schuyler Colfax III (1870-1925) - FAMILY, HIGHER, FIRST/youngest
- Offices: Colfax: U.S. Representative, Indiana 9th district 1855-1869; Speaker of the U.S. House 1863-1869; 17th Vice President 1869-1873. Whig, then Republican (a party founder). Son Schuyler Colfax III: Mayor of South Bend 1898-1902 per the mayors list (the Colfax article says 1898-1901), Republican.
- Facts: Family moved to South Bend in 1841; he edited the Whig-leaning South Bend Free Press from 1842, bought it in 1845 and renamed it the St. Joseph Valley Register. Led House efforts on the Thirteenth Amendment. Colfax Avenue in South Bend is named for him; buried in South Bend City Cemetery. The mayors list notes Colfax III as the "youngest mayor" (the basis is not stated).
- Controversy (factual): A 1872-73 congressional investigation found he accepted a $1,200 check for 20 shares of Credit Mobilier stock from Oakes Ames in 1868; an impeachment resolution failed and he never ran for office again.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schuyler_Colfax ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_South_Bend,_Indiana . Confidence HIGH (Colfax); MED (Colfax III "youngest" claim, single source).

### A2. Alexis Coquillard (1795-1855) - historic note (no office documented)
- Fur trader; the article calls him "the founder of South Bend." Built the city's first mill in 1839 with John Hendricks and John Rush; helped found Notre Dame (1842) with Fr. Edward Sorin. The article lists no public offices and is flagged for thin citations. Include only as a founding-era figure, if at all.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Coquillard . Confidence LOW-MED.

### A3. John Brademas (1927-2016) - TENURE, FIRST, national leadership
- U.S. Rep., Indiana 3rd, 1959-1981 (Democrat); House Majority Whip 1977-81; later NYU president (1981-91/92). Born in Mishawaka. Described as the first Greek-American member of Congress. Helped write federal education/arts legislation, including co-sponsoring the 1965 law creating the NEA and NEH. Lost 1980 reelection to John Hiler. South Bend's main post office is named for him.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brademas . Confidence HIGH.

### A4. John Hiler (b. 1953) - ACCOMP (turning point: ended Brademas's 22-year tenure)
- U.S. Rep., Indiana 3rd, 1981-1991 (Republican), five terms; defeated Brademas in 1980 while Brademas was Majority Whip; lost 1990 to Tim Roemer, a former Brademas staffer. Later GSA deputy administrator 1991-93. Lives in South Bend.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hiler . Confidence HIGH.

### A5. Tim Roemer (b. 1956) - HIGHER
- U.S. Rep., Indiana 3rd, 1991-2003 (Democrat), six terms; Chair, New Democrat Coalition 1997-2001; 9/11 Commission member; U.S. Ambassador to India 2009-2011. Born in South Bend; Penn High School 1975. Ryan Dvorak was a senior aide 1996-2001 (see A13).
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Roemer . Confidence HIGH.

### A6. Joe Kernan (1946-2020) - HIGHER (governor), ACCOMP
- South Bend Controller 1980-84; Mayor 1988-Jan 1997; Lt. Governor 1997-2003; Governor Sept 2003-Jan 2005 (took office on the death of Gov. Frank O'Bannon); Democrat. Navy flight officer shot down over North Vietnam, POW about 11 months. Lost the 2004 governor race to Mitch Daniels (about 53%-45%).
- Controversy (factual): Kernan pledged no city funds would build the College Football Hall of Fame, but after raising under $2 million in corporate donations the city issued municipal bonds for construction (per Wikipedia).
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Kernan_(politician) (the plain /Joe_Kernan URL is a disambiguation page). Confidence HIGH.

### A7. Stephen (Steve) Luecke (b. 1950) - TENURE
- South Bend Common Council (1st District) 1988-97; Mayor Jan 1997-Jan 2012 (Democrat); entered office after Kernan became Lt. Governor, then won 1999, 2003, 2007. The Wikipedia mayors list shows him with the longest tenure (14 years, 363 days). Projects: Morris Performing Arts Center renovation (2000), Eddy Street Commons, Kroc Center, Studebaker-site tech parks (Ignition Park), Green Ribbon Commission (2009), "smart sewer" program. South Bend won All-America City in 2011.
- Controversy/criticism (factual): Newsweek listed South Bend among "America's Dying Cities" in 2011.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Luecke ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_South_Bend,_Indiana . Confidence HIGH.

### A8. Pete Buttigieg (b. 1982) - FIRST/youngest, HIGHER
- Mayor of South Bend Jan 2012-Jan 2020 (Democrat); took office at 29, described by Wikipedia as the youngest incumbent mayor of a U.S. city of 100,000+ at the time; came out as gay in 2015 during his reelection campaign; U.S. Secretary of Transportation Feb 2021-Jan 2025. Programs: Smart Streets (completed 2017), "1,000 Properties in 1,000 Days" (goal reached two months early), 3-1-1 system.
- Controversies (factual): In June 2019 a white South Bend officer shot and killed Eric Logan, a Black man; body cameras were not on, and the mayor's response drew criticism from the police union and some community members. In 2012 he demoted police chief Darryl Boykins (the city's first Black police chief) after a recorded-calls scandal; Boykins sued for racial discrimination and the city settled related suits for over $800,000.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Buttigieg . Confidence HIGH (verify Logan/Boykins details against news before use). Source conflict: Wikipedia's mayors list says it lists "no later offices" for him, while his main article lists Transportation Secretary; the main article is more complete.

### A9. James Mueller (b. 1982) - current office
- Mayor of South Bend since Jan 1, 2020 (Democrat); won 2019 with about 63%; reelected 2023; formerly Buttigieg's chief of staff and director of community investment; Ph.D. oceanography (Delaware). Noted for COVID-19 response, homelessness programs (Motels4Now), police use-of-force policy changes.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mueller_(mayor) . Confidence HIGH.

### A10. Joe Donnelly (b. 1955) - HIGHER
- Mishawaka Marian School Board 1997-2001; U.S. Rep., Indiana 2nd, 2007-2013; U.S. Senator 2013-2019 (Democrat); Ambassador to the Holy See 2022-2024. Lives in Granger. Lost 2018 reelection.
- Controversies (factual, per Wikipedia): 2017 AP report that a family business he had ties to used Mexican labor; he agreed to sell his stock. Changed position on same-sex marriage in 2013.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Donnelly . Confidence HIGH.

### A11. Jackie Walorski (1963-2022) - HIGHER, ACCOMP
- Indiana House (21st district) Jan 2005-Nov 2010 (Republican); U.S. Rep., Indiana 2nd, Jan 2013-Aug 2022; House Ethics Committee ranking member 2021-22. Born in South Bend. Died Aug 3, 2022 in a car crash near Nappanee; Rudy Yakym won the special election. A Mishawaka VA clinic is named for her.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Walorski . Confidence HIGH.

### A12. B. Patrick Bauer (b. 1944) and family - TENURE, FAMILY, FIRST
- Indiana House, 6th district, Nov 1970-Nov 2020 (Democrat), described as the longest-serving member of the Indiana House and the first General Assembly member to serve 50 years; Speaker 2003-05 and 2007-11; House Minority Leader 2011. His father, Burnie Bauer, was a state representative and state senator; daughter Maureen Bauer succeeded him in the 6th district in Nov 2020 (won 2024 with 81% against a Libertarian).
- Controversy (factual): In Feb 2011 he led a 36-member Democratic walkout to block a right-to-work bill (ended March 28, 2011; fines up to $3,500 were threatened); a 2012 boycott failed.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._Patrick_Bauer ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_Bauer . Confidence HIGH (Wikipedia gives two start dates for Maureen Bauer: Nov 4 vs Nov 16, 2020).

### A13. Michael Dvorak and Ryan Dvorak - FAMILY, TENURE
- Michael Dvorak (Democrat): Indiana House 8th district Nov 1986-Nov 2002 (won 1986 by defeating three-term Republican incumbent Lloyd Taylor); St. Joseph County Prosecutor Jan 2003-Jan 2015. Ryan Dvorak (Democrat): succeeded his father in the 8th district in 2002 and holds it per the fetched article (check currency); Assistant Democratic Leader after 2012; lost the 2011 Democratic primary for South Bend mayor.
- Controversy (factual): On Dec 16, 2010 Michael Dvorak announced no criminal charges against Prince Shembo, who had been accused by Lizzy Seeberg nine days before her death. In 2013 he asked for a special prosecutor in a voter petition fraud case; former prosecutor Stanley Levco was appointed (abc57).
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Dvorak ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Dvorak ; https://www.abc57.com/news/dvorak-out-special-prosecutor-appointed-in-voter-petition-fraud-case . Confidence HIGH/MED. Michael is said to have eight children; only Ryan's office-holding is confirmed.

### A14. Craig Fry - TENURE
- Indiana House 5th district (Mishawaka area) 1988-2012 (Democrat); successor Dale DeVon (Republican). 24 years. Ivy Tech apprenticeship director. Sparse biography.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Fry . Confidence MED.

### A15. David Niezgodski and Sheila Niezgodski - FAMILY (spouses), TENURE
- David (Democrat): Indiana House 7th district Nov 2006-Nov 2016; Indiana Senate 10th district since Nov 2016. Sheila Niezgodski: elected to South Bend Common Council District 6 in 2019 (per his article).
- Controversy (factual): Redress South Bend reported on April 12, 2024 sexual harassment allegations dating to 2017; the article says his plumbing company settled with a former employee for $8,000 on condition of non-disclosure (per Wikipedia; verify with Indianapolis Star / South Bend Tribune).
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Niezgodski . Confidence MED (allegation details need verification against news sources before publishing).

### A16. Ryan Mishler (b. 1968) - TENURE (current)
- Indiana Senate 9th district since 2004 (Republican); district spans Kosciusko, Marshall, St. Joseph and Elkhart counties; chairs Appropriations; Bremen funeral director. Unopposed in 2024.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Mishler . Confidence MED (check district boundaries after redistricting).

### A17. Robert "Bob" Beutter (d. Jan 2020 at 84) - TENURE (Mishawaka)
- Mayor of Mishawaka 1984 to 2003 (some sources say 2004); WSBT and the city described him as the longest-serving mayor in Mishawaka's history; five terms. Party not stated in fetched sources. Led the city's purchase of the former Uniroyal plant from the bankruptcy trustee, leading to the Riverwalk and a park named for him. Began as assistant city attorney under Mayor Margaret Prickett.
- Sources: https://wsbt.com/news/local/mishawakas-longest-serving-mayor-dies-at-age-84 ; https://www.abc57.com/news/former-mishawaka-mayor-robert-c-beutter-dies . Confidence MED-HIGH (end year conflict 2003 vs 2004).

### A18. Margaret Prickett - TENURE, possible FIRST (Mishawaka)
- Mishawaka mayor elected 1963; served four terms (about 16 years) until losing in 1979 to Democrat Bob Kovach. A search snippet did not confirm she was the first woman mayor of Mishawaka. Wikipedia's list of first women mayors credits Mary Jancosek Bercik of Whiting as Indiana's first (1957). Party presumed Republican (snippet says she "carried the whole Republican ticket") - LOW-MED. Needs primary verification.
- Sources: https://journalreview.com/stories/cracking-the-mayoral-glass-ceiling,245697 (search result, not opened); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first_women_mayors_(20th_century) .

### A19. Jeff Rea and Dave Wood (Mishawaka)
- Dave Wood (Republican): current Mishawaka mayor; took over in 2010 to finish Rea's term; described as Mishawaka's 21st mayor. Jeff Rea: preceded Wood; resigned to lead the South Bend Regional Chamber of Commerce; the city dedicated a Kamm Island pavilion to him on July 14, 2026. Rea's start year and party not confirmed.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishawaka,_Indiana ; https://mishawaka.in.gov/news/city-of-mishawaka-dedicates-new-kamm-island-pavilion-to-former-mayor-jeff-rea/ ; search result summaries. Confidence MED.

### A20. J. Chester Allen - FIRST
- A South Bend Tribune piece (via Yahoo) calls him the first African American to represent South Bend in state government, "the first person elected to serve on the South Bend Common Council" (the article's wording; looks inconsistent with a 19th-century council, so verify), first Black appointee to the school board, and first president of the local bar association. Another search snippet says he was elected to the Indiana House in 1938. Ran a law firm with his wife Elizabeth Fletcher. Party not stated.
- Source: https://www.yahoo.com/news/black-history-month-j-chester-110429823.html . Confidence LOW-MED; dates not given.

### A21. Eugenia Braboy and Beverly/Charlotte Pfeifer - FIRST (South Bend Common Council)
- A 2003 city resolution names Eugenia Braboy as the first African American to hold a district council seat (1984-1987) and the first African-American woman on the council. Pfeifer served 1996-2007 and was the council's first African American president (2000), per a WVPE profile. First name conflicts between sources (Beverly vs Charlotte).
- Sources: https://docs.southbendin.gov/WebLink/0/doc/38214/Page2.aspx ; https://www.wvpe.org/commentary/2019-03-07/women-making-history-charlotte-pfeifer (search result summaries, not opened). Confidence LOW-MED.

### A22. Sherry Bolden Simpson - FIRST (current)
- South Bend Common Council 5th District, took office Jan 1, 2024; her city page says she is the first Democrat elected to that seat since 1962 and the first woman/first Black woman to hold it.
- Source: https://southbendin.gov/official/sherry-bolden-simpson/ . Confidence MED (self-described, city page).

### A23. Women-majority South Bend Common Council - FIRST
- Search-result headlines report the Common Council would be seated with a female majority in January (year not confirmed). A separate headline reports an all-Democratic council swearing in on New Year's Day.
- Sources: https://www.wndu.com/content/news/History-made-as-SB-Common-Council-will-be-women-majority-564582871.html ; https://abc57.com/news/south-bends-all-democratic-council-swears-in-new-years-day . Confidence LOW (headlines only).

### A24. Other South Bend mayors (from Wikipedia's list; notes only)
- William G. George, first mayor after 1865 incorporation (Republican). Fred W. Keller, 1914-18, the only Independent. Jesse Pavey 1938-45 (seven-year term, among the longest before 1972). Edward Voorde died in office Sept 1960 (successor Frank Bruggner). Lloyd Allen (Republican) 1964-72 was the last Republican; the page says every mayor since 1972 has been a Democrat. Roger Parent 1980-88.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_South_Bend,_Indiana . Confidence MED (the page notes inconsistencies: Jerry Miller 1972-75 vs 1972-85; Longley 1888-92 vs 1884-88; "Fogarty/Fogary").
