## 1. County structure (IC 36-2-2-4(c) / 36-2-3-4 opt-in)

**Statute text (secondary mirror, FindLaw):** IC 36-2-2-4(c) "applies to a county having a population of more than 185,000 and less than 300,000 that opts in ... by passing a resolution by a majority vote of its executive body not later than September 1, 2023." If the executive body does not opt in by then, "the county shall be governed by the general provisions of this chapter." Under the opt-in, "the executive shall divide the county into three (3) single-member districts." The subsection cross-references IC 36-2-3-4(c) (council). Source: https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-36-local-government/in-code-sect-36-2-2-4/ (Justia returned 403).

**Whether St. Joseph County passed the opt-in resolution: NOT FOUND.** No ordinance, resolution or news report located (1 search + statute fetch). Evidence consistent with an opt-in structure:
- Council roster is nine single-member districts A-I (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Joseph_County,_Indiana).
- South Bend's 2023 suit says the county has nine council members, each voter votes for one of nine (ABC57, https://abc57.com/news/city-of-south-bend-names-county-commissioners-in-election-lawsuit).
- 2021 reporting: a 2021 state-law change applying only to St. Joseph County let commissioners draw maps, with council districts drawn within the three commissioner districts (WVPE Dec 2021, https://www.wvpe.org/indiana-news/2021-12-30/st-joseph-county-council-overrides-commissioners-veto-of-democratic-redistricting-plan; search snippet only).
- A search snippet describing statute text says counties of 250,000-270,000 elect commissioners from three districts and nine council members from districts nested within them (via https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-36-local-government/in-code-sect-36-2-3-4/; not verified by me against full text). This is an older population-range provision, distinct from the 185,000-300,000 opt-in in (c). The two should be told apart when drafting.

**Commissioners:** 3, elected by district (not county-wide). Lake and Marion are the only other counties that do (ABC57 Apr 22, 2026, URL in item 2).

**Council (Wikipedia roster, undated, probably pre-Nov 2026; verify before use):** A Joseph Thomas (R); B Amy Drake (R); C Dan Schaetzle, President (R); D Jenn Shabazz (D); E Diana Hess (D); F Randy Figg (R); G Mark Catanzarite, Pres. Pro Tem (D); H Bryan Tanner, VP (D); I Andy Rutten (R). Nine seats, so "9 districts" and not "6 + 3" is supported by Wikipedia and the South Bend suit coverage. This agrees with fact-check item 7.

## 7. Running for office mechanics
**2027 municipal (Indiana Election Division, 2026 Election Calendar Election Administrator's Edition, rev. March 2026, pp. 101-107; https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/files/2026-Election-Calendar-Election-Administrators-Edition-Revised-March-2026.pdf):**
- Municipal primary: Tuesday, May 4, 2027 (calendar text "special election ... May 4, 2027" for public questions; also IC statute via search). General: Tuesday, November 2, 2027.
- Deadline: Friday, Feb 5, 2027, by noon, to file a declaration of candidacy (CAN-42) with the county election board for a major-party nomination to a city office, or a town office in a town of 3,500+ (IC 3-8-2-4; 3-8-2-6; 3-8-9-5), with a Statement of Economic Interests (CAN-12). File where the district's largest share of population lies.
- Opening day: Wed, Jan 6, 2027 is stated for towns under 3,500 (declaration, CAN-42/CAN-16) and for minor-party/independent town petitions (CAN-44, CAN-45). The specific opening line for city offices was NOT read; assume the same only after confirming.
- Write-in declarations and withdrawal deadline (Feb 12, 2027, for small-town major-party candidates) appear in the same calendar.
